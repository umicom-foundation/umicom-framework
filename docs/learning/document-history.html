Owning source and selection history

The document coordinator owns Undo and Redo. An internal history entry owns complete source text and, when available, the caret and selection for that exact text. Metadata travels with the entry through eviction and stack transfer, so another document or a newer draft cannot silently supply its coordinates.

Integrate a native typing group

  1. At the outermost toolkit user-action boundary, synchronize earlier draft changes with UmiDocumentCoordinatorSyncDocument.
  2. Call UmiDocumentCoordinatorPrepareEdit before typing changes the native text or selection. Keep its owned capture until the group ends.
  3. Publish the complete accepted text and selection to the existing document view model as usual.
  4. Call UmiDocumentCoordinatorCommitTyping on the coordinator owner thread. It checks document identity, saved identity and the unchanged underlying store before recording the group.
  5. Always destroy the capture. A successful commit consumes it, including an unchanged-text group. Failure leaves the visible draft available for recovery and records no new history step.

The GTK adapter follows this sequence for its outermost begin-user-action and end-user-action signals. It avoids refreshing a buffer during end-user-action dispatch. A retained buffer releases its capture safely after its binding disappears.

Explicit commands and source sets

Existing document edit publication captures the old selection before changing text. When earlier typing has not been synchronized, that older stored state remains a separate text-only step; the command's immediate departure draft keeps its known position. Reviewed source sets capture each member independently and allocate nothing during final history transfer.

Undo restores the target entry's positions. The outgoing view supplies positions for Redo. Moving between history actions deliberately affects the next return position. Selected ranges are represented as a byte start and length, not a directional pair of toolkit marks.

Boundaries and limits

Positions must fit the entry's text and must not split a UTF-8 character or CRLF pair. Text-only entries retain the earlier clamping behavior, extended to respect CRLF. Count and byte-budget eviction rules remain unchanged: at most 32 entries and 16 MiB of text including terminators per stack. Closing releases history. This is not persistent crash recovery.

Extend the coordinator rather than creating a second history stack in an application. Add coverage for pending typing, no-change edits, read-only state, Unicode boundaries, stack eviction and destroyed native owners when introducing another edit route.
